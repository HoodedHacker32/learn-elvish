Lam Edhellen, the translator that tells you how far to trust it

Type English and get Sindarin or Quenya, or paste Elvish and read it in English. Every result says exactly which parts are Tolkien’s and which are not, and when a sentence needs something Tolkien never left, it refuses and says why.

  1. lam“tongue, language”. Lam Edhellen is “the Elvish tongue”.

“I will sing to the moon.” Rated built from Tolkien’s words, with its Tengwar, how to say it, and the closest lines Tolkien actually wrote.

Five ratings, from certain to refused

Every result carries one of these, in the translator’s own words:

  1. Tolkien’s exact wordsA line he wrote, quoted with its source.
  2. The Elvish way to say itNot word for word: what his Elves would say instead, like mae govannen for “nice to meet you”, with its literal meaning.
  3. Built from Tolkien’s wordsEvery word his, joined with grammar he showed. Composed, not quoted.
  4. Partly reconstructedReadable, with each piece that is not his listed.
  5. Can’t translate this reliablyIt needs grammar or words Tolkien never left. You are told why, and offered a way to say it that works.

Also inside: every one of Tolkien’s known Elvish lines, searchable; a lexicon of thousands of words, each with its source; Tengwar for every result; and a plain pronunciation guide with no phonetic symbols.

Learn here, then say your own

The course and the translator share one checked vocabulary and one grammar. Learn mae govannen in a lesson; then ask Lam Edhellen how to greet your own party in your own words.
