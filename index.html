Is Elvish a real language?

Real enough to learn. Tolkien began inventing Elvish languages as a young man and was still revising them when he died in 1973.4 Sindarin, the Elvish spoken in The Lord of the Rings, and Quenya, the ancient tongue of Galadriel’s lament, both have sounds, grammar and thousands of words.

He never finished either. There is no complete dictionary, and plenty of everyday words were never written. People who write Elvish today fill those gaps with new words of their own.5 Pedo Edhellen teaches what Tolkien left and tells you plainly whenever a word is not his.

What the names mean

  1. Eldamo, the lexicon this course draws on, sorts his work into stages: Early Qenya from the 1910s, Middle Quenya and Noldorin from the 1930s, and the Quenya and Sindarin of 1950 to 1973.
  2. Called Neo-Elvish. Eldamo marks every such word, and so does every screen of this course.

Lessons that stick, at your pace

Each lesson teaches a few words or one piece of grammar, then asks you for them, because remembering is what makes them last. Everything you learn returns on a widening schedule. There are no hearts to lose and no league to fall out of, and your run of days has five rest days built into every week.

There is a Sindarin course, a Quenya course, and a Roleplay phrasebook for anyone giving an Elf character real lines at the table.
