What the names mean

Every name here is real Elvish, and every word in it is Tolkien’s own. Sindarin puts the describing word after the thing it describes, so “Elvish tongue” comes out as “tongue Elvish”.

  1. eneth“name”, in Tolkien’s Sindarin. Eneth nín is “my name”.

“Speak Elvish!”

  • pedospeak!2

The course. A command, because Tolkien’s most famous Elvish words are one: the inscription on the door of Moria tells travellers to speak.

  1. From pedo mellon a minno, “speak, friend, and enter”. The Fellowship of the Ring, Book II, ch. 4.
  2. From Gandalf’s annon edhellen, “Elvish gate”, in the same chapter.

Sindarin



SIN-dah-rin

“Grey-elven”

A Quenya word: the language of the Sindar, the Grey-elves, who stayed in Middle-earth. It is the Elvish heard most often in The Lord of the Rings, and the first course in the app.

From the Quenya Sinda, “Grey-elf”. Meanings on this page are as recorded in Eldamo.

Quenya



KWEN-yah

“Elvish, High-elven”

In its oldest sense simply “speech”. The ancient tongue of the High Elves, kept in Middle-earth for lore and song much as Latin was kept in Europe.

Tolkien himself called Quenya an “Elven-latin”, in the appendices to The Lord of the Rings.

Tengwar



TENG-wahr

“Letters”

The plural of tengwa, “letter, sign”: the script Tolkien designed for his languages. Each consonant is a letter and each vowel a small mark written over one.

One tengwa, many tengwar: Quenya plurals often end in -r.
